[WIP] Somewhere City

July 13, 2026

See more

The album, 'Somewhere City' by Origami Angel is their first debut LP that redefined the very concept of 5th-wave emo concept albums. This essay is a reflection and dissection's of Origami Angel's album and the significance of it. In the modern age, the wave of the pre-defined "emo" stereotype is dead. Most common people would think of a Gerard Way 2007 sweeping side bangs and baggy jeans as well as black eye-liner. Couple that with a dark and cringe attitude about hating the color pink, and we have the common "emo" stereotype. However, that main-stream look is now at the past. Emo as a genre is now about combining genres, styles, and other creative outputs.

Welcome to....
To start off the album, 'Welcome to...", as the title suggests, someone or something is taking us somewhere. The intro riff creates a nostalgic and calming aura just like visiting an old childhood town. Then, the first few words of the song reassures the listeners. Its something we all want to hear from a friend or family member: "When I feel so lost. Without a plan or a purpose, a cent or a dollar, or even a cause. I know where I need to go to feel better, Somewhere that I know that I'll always belong. " Its hinting on a place. Somewhere that the speaker feel safe when feeling like they don't have a purpose, or cause. A place of reassurance. It only appears when it needs you the most.
This really reminds me of the "Room of Requirement" in Harry Potter, the room only appears only when needed. If you watched the Harry Potter Films, you know that when Dolores Umbridge banned practical magic, Neville Longbottom discovered the room to give the students a safe space to practice defensive spells and the students used it against Umbridge. This feels the same. Moments later in the song, the line "The city never lets me down" plays over and over again. The harmony is very catchy and feeling out like the speaker is singing along with others as they invite others in this "city" that only appears only when we feel down, rejected, or purposeless.

24 Hr Drive-Thru
The title describes itself. Its about a song inviting a friend to a drive-through when said friend is feeling sad. The song is very catchy. With a very funky and fast instrumental part near the end. Its a summer, road-trip type of song. The unique thing about this song is that it references real world places. "All you need to do is call me. And I'll take Kenilworth Avenue to 193 and we can get fries under dark stormy skies". Kenilworth Avenue is a road in College Park, Maryland, in the DMV area of the United States that Origami Angel is based out of. The writer is taking Kenilworth to get to Maryland’s Route 193, a major highway that cuts through the state and is a primary access point to the University of Maryland. Along the way the speaker seems to imply that they will pick up the person the song is speaking to, and they will then drive together to enjoy some late-night cuisine at the song’s namesake, a 24 Hour drive thru.

My first week in college went great. Great as in nothing much happened. I still do not know 99% of the people in my class. Although I don't intend to, I'll be normal to know them as the school year goes on. I didn't really care that much. I don't intend to. I want to stay out of the spotlight and do nothing. Maybe its trauma that happened from Grade 11-12, but the more I'm out of it, the safer I feel. Thankfully I found out that I have a friend in the same course and class as me, Dale. Man, shoutout to Dale. I would have been lost without his extroverted ass. I'm forever in dept to him.

Following the reasons I want to stay out of the spotlight: In college, no one knew who I am (for the most part), so I can be anything I want to be (Finally). For most of my senior High School years, I just wanted to disappear. Gone. Everything hurts so much inside that classroom. Although I grew as a person (for the most part). I lost so much in just those two years: myself. I was so confident, cheerful, without a care in the world. Mental health drains you. I didn't really realize it back then, but it was a decline from the moment I step foot in those walls. Something I learned is that depression, self-hatred, and problems with myself does not happen on an instant. It doesn't happen on a single break-up text, or a hurtful insult. It happens overtime. And when you do notice it, it hurts so much.

Do I regret what the fuck happened in the last two years? If I was to answer that the day of my high school graduation, absolutely yes. Because quite frankly I was stupidly and hilariously blinded by love. If I was to go back and meet myself on February 15th, 2025, I would beat the living shit out of me.

Summer vacation started and I locked myself inside my room. Didn't go out. Just by myself. I told myself I would one, focus on my part-time job, and two, work on myself. I declined every offer I got to hang out with friends (no offence) and left myself inside. I just didn't want to be anybody anymore. If I was going to be in college, I had to learn to be myself. I want to explore more about this topic but unfortunately this is getting out of topic, so I am going to leave this in a separate post.

College is such a mixed bag. I see myself in some of my classmates. Some who raises their hand in every question to get as much points as possible, I used to be like that in my grade 8-9 classes when I started taking school seriously. Some, having an intimidating stare and would rather sit in the back of the room and talk to no one, me as of lately. Maybe I just view myself that has gone through a lot, but I frankly just don't give a fuck anymore. Being myself and not letting anyone change me is my safety rule. I try to ignore the aspects I see in someone that I relate to or envy. Because those are some of the things that makes me want to be something that I don't want to. I want to be myself, someone who choses what they are. Because most of the time, it's me VS me. Most of my life after getting a degree, job, or even a family, I'm going to be alone making those little chooses. I want to see a future where I see myself.
